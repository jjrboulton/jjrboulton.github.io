Independent computational project In development

Anum: An Artificial Life Simulation

Julia · Artificial life · Evolutionary computation · Computational cognition

Anum is an experimental two-dimensional environment for investigating the evolution of adaptive behaviour, multicellular organisation and embodied intelligence. Its central approach is to study how increasingly coordinated behaviour might emerge through evolution in organisms composed of interacting cells.

Research questions

  • Under what conditions can coordinated multicellular behaviour emerge through evolutionary processes?
  • How do physical constraints shape the evolution of locomotion and adaptive behaviour?
  • Can interpretable computational architectures support increasingly sophisticated sensorimotor behaviour?
  • What kinds of internal organisation might support world-modelling and self-modelling?

Model and implementation

  • A two-dimensional physical environment in which anisotropic drag makes directional movement consequential.
  • Cells with sensors and actuators that mediate interactions with their environment.
  • Genetically encoded fuzzy-logic circuits, using Zadeh-style operators, to determine cell responses.
  • Evolutionary processes intended to support the emergence of multicellular organisation and genetic exchange.

A longer-term research objective is to investigate whether coordinated navigation and other adaptive capabilities can emerge from local cellular interactions, and whether the resulting control structures can be analysed in human-interpretable terms. This is an objective for investigation, not a result claimed to have been demonstrated.

A Physicalist Account of Phenomenal Consciousness

Philosophy of mind · Metaphysics · Phenomenal concepts

Research developed in collaboration with Dr Douglas Ian Campbell at the University of Canterbury, concerning a physicalist account of phenomenal consciousness. The account brings together several theoretical approaches:

  • A quotational account of phenomenal concepts.
  • A relational account of qualia.
  • A simulation-based account of social cognition.
  • An essential-indexical account of psychophysical identity statements.

The project examines how this combination might address familiar challenges for physicalist theories, including conceivability arguments, the inverted spectrum, Nagel's bat, and the explanatory and epistemic gaps.

A related computational project in Racket explores aspects of a cognitive architecture informed by the theory. The philosophical account and its computational exploration should be understood as related but distinct work.
